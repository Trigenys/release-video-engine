# Validation offers and outreach

Issues: #1, #7

## Purpose

The concierge pilot must validate willingness to pay for the **release-to-video outcome**, not generic AI video and not the underlying renderer.

Infrastructure cost is already low enough that pilot pricing should test perceived value, buying friction and workflow ownership rather than cost-plus pricing.

## Market context — 2026-10-01

Public self-serve video tools currently span roughly:

- HeyGen Creator: $29/month;
- HeyGen Business: $149/month plus additional seats;
- Descript Creator: $35/month monthly;
- Descript Business: $65/person/month monthly.

Release Video Engine is narrower than these general-purpose editors, but the concierge pilot includes prospect-specific release interpretation, brand adaptation and finished release assets. The test should therefore price the **outcome**, not compare feature-for-feature with an editor seat.

These market references are context only. They do not determine the offer.

## Price anchors to test

Use two explicit anchors across the 20-prospect pilot.

### Anchor A — low-friction

- **Launch Pack:** $49 one-off
  - one public release;
  - 9:16, 1:1 and 16:9;
  - one language;
  - one light revision.
- **Monthly Release Automation:** $99/month
  - up to 4 releases/month;
  - three formats per release;
  - one reusable brand template.
- **Brand Template Setup:** $149 one-time when a custom visual system is required.

Purpose: test whether teams will pay at all when the price is easy to approve.

### Anchor B — stronger value test

- **Launch Pack:** $99 one-off.
- **Monthly Release Automation:** $199/month for up to 4 releases.
- **Brand Template Setup:** $249 one-time.

Purpose: test whether the release workflow is valuable enough to sit above generic creator-tool pricing.

## Assignment rule

Alternate anchors across qualified prospects rather than changing the price after hearing a reaction.

- odd-numbered prospects: Anchor A;
- even-numbered prospects: Anchor B.

Do not discount during the first response. Capture the objection first.

## What counts as willingness-to-pay evidence

Strong evidence:

- asks how to pay;
- asks for invoice/procurement;
- requests a paid pilot;
- requests pricing for more releases;
- asks whether the offer supports their release cadence;
- asks for a call specifically to discuss rollout.

Medium evidence:

- explicitly says the price is reasonable;
- asks for a demo after seeing the artifact;
- asks for a lower/higher package;
- names a budget owner or procurement constraint.

Weak evidence:

- generic praise;
- likes/reactions;
- "interesting";
- asks technical questions without a buying question.

## Discovery questions

Do not turn first contact into an interview form. Use one question at a time.

Useful questions:

1. How do you currently announce a release after engineering ships it?
2. How often do you ship something worth announcing?
3. Who usually turns release notes into launch/social content?
4. What makes video hard enough that it gets skipped?
5. Would you rather buy this per launch or as an always-on monthly workflow?
6. If this reliably produced publish-ready assets for each release, what budget would feel normal?

## Formbricks — prospect #1

Source release: **6.0.1 — 2026-09-29**

Artifact status:

- tailored Remotion preview: produced;
- tailored HyperFrames preview: produced;
- media-heavy parity output: produced and visually reviewed;
- public release data only;

Assigned price anchor: **Anchor A**.

### Primary outreach

> Hey Formbricks team — I used your 6.0.1 release notes to prototype what your release could look like as a short branded launch video instead of another changelog post.
>
> I built the preview from public release data only — no partnership implied.
>
> I’m testing a small release-to-video service for teams that ship frequently: $49 for a one-off launch pack (9:16, 1:1 and 16:9) or $99/month for up to four releases.
>
> Would this actually save your team enough launch work to be useful, or is video not a bottleneck for you today?

### Short version

> Made a short release-video concept from Formbricks 6.0.1 using only the public release notes. I’m testing $49/launch or $99/month for teams that ship often. Is release marketing/video actually painful enough for this to be useful to you?

### Follow-up after no response

> Quick follow-up on the Formbricks 6.0.1 concept — no need for a sales call. Even a “we would never pay for this because ___” would be genuinely useful while I’m validating the workflow.

## Outreach handling

For every contact, record:

- exact artifact shown;
- assigned anchor;
- channel;
- date;
- response verbatim or faithful summary;
- objection;
- current workflow;
- release frequency;
- owner of release marketing;
- preferred buying model;
- pricing signal;
- next step.

Do not count a prospect as contacted until the message was actually sent through a real channel.

## Pilot decision rule

After 20 contacts:

- **GO:** at least 8 substantive responses, at least 3 demo/pricing follow-ups and at least 1 paid-pilot/procurement step.
- **NARROW:** response quality is good but concentrated in one subsegment, cadence, company size or buying model.
- **STOP / reframe:** fewer than 3 meaningful buying signals after 20 well-qualified, artifact-backed contacts.

Do not add more self-serve product surface before making this decision.
